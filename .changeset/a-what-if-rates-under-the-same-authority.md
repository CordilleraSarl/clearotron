A what-if now rates the risk under the customer's framework, not the house default

Asking "what if X were true" about a finished report re-ran the analysis under the
generic in-house rating framework instead of the customer's own, and said nothing about
having done so. The answer that came back therefore mixed two changes — the assumption
being tested and a different rating authority — with no way for the reader to tell them
apart. A what-if now inherits the profile the original run was rated under, refuses if
that customer can no longer be found rather than substituting one, and states on the
result which authority it used.
