# Field doctrine — pharmaceuticals (spec-48 C4)

Force-read by synthesis when the matter is pharma-shaped (Nice class 5 anywhere in scope, or
pharma-shaped goods text). These rules SHARPEN how the standard framework applies to medicine
marks — they never replace the doctrine tables in `risk-framework.md` and they never change how a
Level/Composite is computed; they change what counts as proximity, use-evidence weight, and
confusion likelihood **inside** that framework. The refutation review verifies they were honoured
on pharma matters; a violation is a flagged correction.

**Validation:** this module ships behind a PR checkbox for the senior lawyer — treat its rules
as binding once merged; propose edits through them, not ad hoc.

## 1. Therapeutic-area goods discipline

Class 5 is not one field. Goods proximity for medicine marks runs on **therapeutic area and
patient population**, not on formulation or distribution channel:

- Same therapeutic area (two cardiovascular drugs, two anti-inflammatories) ⇒ treat goods as
  PROXIMATE even when formulation, prescription status, or channel differ. Doctors prescribe and
  pharmacists dispense across those lines daily.
- Different therapeutic areas can still be proximate when the patient population overlaps
  (paediatric anything; oncology adjacents) or when one is an input/companion to the other
  (a drug and its delivery device, a drug and the diagnostic that gates it).
- Rx vs OTC is a WEAK separator — switching is common, and both meet in the same pharmacy.
- Veterinary ↔ human medicine: same therapeutic logic applies (compounds and brands cross over);
  do not off-field a veterinary mark against a human-medicine mark (or vice versa) on the species
  line alone.

## 2. No-use never softens a pharma risk

A registered pharma mark with **no visible market use is normal, not weak**: development pipelines
run 5–10 years pre-launch behind exactly such registrations, and pharma houses clear and register
years ahead of approval. Therefore:

- Never lower a pharma finding's read because "no products found under this mark".
- Never present a use-negative as if it reduced enforcement appetite — pharma owners police
  pipelines, not shelves.
- The use meter can honestly read `not-confirmed`; the RISK meters must not inherit softness
  from it on a pharma matter. (The generic no-use rule in `synthesis-rules.md` applies with extra
  force here.)

## 3. Practitioner and pharmacist confusion — including look-alike/sound-alike error

The confusion audience for medicine marks includes **prescribers and dispensers under time
pressure**, not just end consumers, and the harm mode includes medication error:

- Weigh **handwriting look-alikes** (scripts still travel handwritten or transcribed): letter
  shapes that collapse when written — a/o, e/i, rn/m, l/t crossings — can make two marks
  confusable on paper that look distinct on screen.
- Weigh **verbal/telephone order sound-alikes** independently of spelling.
- A confusion route that could plausibly cause a DISPENSING error is a risk-RAISING fact for the
  likelihood analysis (safety-adjacent confusion is exactly what offices and courts weigh more
  heavily for class 5); say so explicitly in the read when it applies.

## 4. Regulatory-name adjacency (context, not a clearance)

INN stems and recommended names are a REGULATORY screen, not a trademark clearance — if the mark
skirts an INN stem (e.g. a protected suffix/prefix family), note it as its own open item for
specialist counsel; never fold it silently into the trademark read, and never claim an INN screen
that was not actually run.
